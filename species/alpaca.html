Alpaca

Vicugna pacos

Overview

Alpaca appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Alpaca — ESA: Not Listed; CITES: N/A. [1]

Native range

The native range of Lama pacos includes the central and southern Andes from Peru to Argentina at elevations of up to 4800 meters. [2]

Size

55–65 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

20 years (captivity, average); 5 to 10 years (wild, typical); 25.8 years (captivity, average). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation 242–345 days; average 190.5 days; offspring per birth: 1; females mature at 12 to 15 months. [2]

Habitat needs

Alpacas inhabit the Andean Altiplano, i.e. the Andean high plateau, preferably near wet areas. ADW biomes: savanna or grassland, mountains. Elevation range 1000–4800 m. [2]

Natural diet

Lama pacos is a strict grazer. In a highland region of Chile, Castellano et al. reported that the alpaca diet was dominated by grasses such as Festuca nardifolia, Deschampsia caespitosa, and Agrostis tolucensis, cushion plants Oxychloe andina, bunch grasses Festuca orthophylla, and the woody shrub…. ADW diet type: herbivore, folivore. [2]

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [3]

Conservation status

IUCN Red List: Domesticated form (not assessed separately); vicuña, its wild ancestor (Vicugna vicugna): Least Concern. [4]

General information, not legal advice. USDA APHIS licensing under the Animal Welfare Act applies to dealers and exhibitors; check USDA and your state wildlife and animal-health agencies before buying, moving or selling. [5][6]
