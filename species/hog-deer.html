Hog Deer

Axis porcinus

Overview

Hog Deer appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Hog Deer — ESA: Not Listed; CITES: Appendix I. [1]

Native range

Axis porcinus has a native geographic range throughout India, including the Himalayan foothill zone and Southeast Asia, including Burma and Thailand. [2]

Size

36–50 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

20 years (wild, average); 20 years (captivity, average); 10 years (wild, average). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation average 8 months; offspring per birth: 1–2; females mature at 8-12 months. [2]

Habitat needs

Axis porcinus appears to prefer dense forests; however, they are often observed in clearings, grasslands and occasionally wet grasslands. ADW biomes: forest. [2]

Fencing

Texas Parks & Wildlife states game fences at least 8 ft high (woven-wire or high-tensile) are the only fully reliable deer-exclusion fence. That is the common high-fence standard for deer and antelope; a species-specific minimum for this animal was not found in a primary source. [3]

Natural diet

Hog deer feed nocturnally. They both graze and browse, but seem to prefer grazing. ADW diet type: herbivore, folivore. [2]

Feed & supplementation

For supplementation TPWD recommends a 16–20% crude-protein pelleted feed rather than corn (corn is only 7–10% CP). AgriLife cites ~6–7% CP for rumen maintenance and 13–16% CP for growth, antler production and reproduction (white-tailed deer data; applied to exotic deer by analogy). Mineral program: set with a nutritionist/vet; no verified species figure. [4][5][6]

Common health issues & parasites

Haemonchus contortus and Longistrongylus dominate abomasal worm burdens in free-ranging exotic antelope in Texas (sable, addax, blesbok studied); captive wild ruminants show anthelmintic resistance, so use fecal egg counts and fecal egg-count reduction tests instead of routine blanket deworming. [7][8]

Vet care & vaccination

No vaccines are labeled for this species in the US; any vaccination protocol is extra-label and should be set by a veterinarian experienced with exotic hoofstock. (No specific protocol was verified for this page.)

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [9]

Conservation status

IUCN Red List: Endangered. [10]

Legal notes (general info, not legal advice)

CITES Appendix I (per the EWA list); international trade needs CITES permits. General information, not legal advice. USDA APHIS licensing under the Animal Welfare Act applies to dealers and exhibitors; check USDA and your state wildlife and animal-health agencies before buying, moving or selling. [1][11][12]
