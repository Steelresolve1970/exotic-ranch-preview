White Rhinoceros

Ceratotherium simum

Overview

White Rhinoceros appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Rhinoceros (White) — ESA: Threatened; CITES: Appendix II. [1]

Native range

Historically, northwestern Uganda, southern Chad, south-western Sudan, the eastern part of center African Republic, and northeastern Democratic Republic of the Congo were the native places for northern white rhinoceroses ( Ceratotherium simum cottoni ). [2]

Size

1,000–3,600 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

46 to 50 years (wild, average); 39 to 43 years (wild, typical); 27 to 30 years (captivity, typical). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation 530–550 days; offspring per birth: 1; females mature at 3 to 5 years. [2]

Habitat needs

Typical habitat for white rhinocerose includes dense forests, savannas, and woodlands with grassy openings. ADW biomes: savanna or grassland, forest, scrub forest. [2]

Natural diet

White rhinoceroses are strictly herbivores. Their general diets include thick bush covers and short grasses. ADW diet type: herbivore, folivore. [2]

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [3]

Conservation status

IUCN Red List: Near Threatened. [4]
