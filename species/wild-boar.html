Wild Boar (Feral Hog)

Sus scrofa

Overview

Wild Boar (Feral Hog) appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Hog (Feral) — ESA: Not Listed; CITES: N/A. [1]

Native range

Wild boars ( Sus scrofa ) are a cosmopolitan species. They originated in Europe and Asia, but were widely introduced to North America and are considered an invasive species in the southeastern United States and California. [2]

Size

66–272 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

12-13 years (wild, range); 27 years (captivity, range); 2 to 9-10 years (wild, typical). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation 108–120 days; offspring per birth: 5; females mature at 10 months. [2]

Habitat needs

Given their wide distribution, wild boars can be found in a variety of habitats. They may inhabit grassy savanna areas, wooded forests, agricultural areas, shrublands and marshy swamplands. ADW biomes: savanna or grassland, forest. [2]

Natural diet

Wild boars are omnivorous. They predominantly eat plant matter, particularly crops, fruits, nuts (mast), roots, and green plants. ADW diet type: omnivore. [2]

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [3]

IUCN Red List: Least Concern. [4]
