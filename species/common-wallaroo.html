Common Wallaroo

Macropus robustus

Overview

Common Wallaroo appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Kangaroo (Wallaroo) — ESA: Not Listed; CITES: Appendix II. [1]

Native range

Hill wallaroos are found only in western Australia in New South Wales, the Pilbara district, and Queensland. [2]

Size

18–42 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

24 years (wild, range); 22 years (captivity, range); 20 to 24 years (wild, typical). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation 30–38 days; average 36 days; offspring per birth: 1; females mature at 21 to 24 months. [2]

Habitat needs

Hill wallaroos are usually found on or in rocky hills, caves, and rock formations with large overhangs, anything that can provide shelter from the intense heat during the day. ADW biomes: savanna or grassland, scrub forest. Elevation range 0–500 m. [2]

Natural diet

Herbivorous hill wallaroos mostly graze on soft-textured grasses and shrubs. All foraging occurs mainly within the home range. ADW diet type: herbivore, folivore. [2]

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [3]

Conservation status

IUCN Red List: Assessed under the current name Osphranter robustus: Least Concern. [4]
