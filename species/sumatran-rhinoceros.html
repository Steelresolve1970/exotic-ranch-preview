Sumatran Rhinoceros

Dicerorhinus sumatrensis

Overview

Sumatran Rhinoceros appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Rhinoceros (Sumatran) — ESA: Endangered; CITES: Appendix I. [1]

Native range

Sumatran rhinoceroses ( Dicerorhinus sumatrensis ) had a historic range that included Assam, Burma and Indochina to Malaysia, and areas in northern Sumatra and northwestern Borneo. [2]

Size

600–1,000 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

32.8 years (captivity, range); 30 to 45 years (wild, typical); 35 years (wild, average). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation 15–16 months; offspring per birth: 1; females mature at 6 to 7 years. [2]

Habitat needs

Sumatran rhinoceroses live in upland forests around Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra, and Borneo, at elevations up to 2,000 m above sea level. ADW biomes: savanna or grassland, forest. [2]

Natural diet

Sumatran rhinoceroses are browsers, eating small trees, shrubs, and herbs. Sumatran rhinoceroses do not have specific periods of feeding; they alternate the times that they feed depending on when they are traveling. ADW diet type: herbivore, folivore, lignivore. [2]

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [3]

Conservation status

IUCN Red List: Critically Endangered. [4]
