Red-necked (Bennett's) Wallaby

Macropus rufogriseus

Overview

Red-necked (Bennett's) Wallaby appears on the Exotic Wildlife Association species list as Kangaroo (Wallaby) — ESA: Not Listed; CITES: Appendix II. [1]

Native range

Red-necked wallabies inhabit the coastal forests of eastern and southeastern Australia and are especially common in Queensland, northeastern New South Wales and Tasmania. [2]

Size

13.8–18.6 kg (ADW range). [2]

Lifespan

15 years (wild, average); 18.5 years (wild, average); 15.2 years (captivity, average). [2]

Breeding & gestation

Gestation average 29 days; offspring per birth: 1. [2]

Habitat needs

Red-necked wallabies inhabit the eucalypt forests with moderate shrub cover and open areas nearby, and also populate the tall coastal heath communities. ADW biomes: savanna or grassland, chaparral, forest. [2]

Natural diet

Red-necked wallabies are essentially grazers, consuming largely grasses and herbs. Juicy roots during dry spells supply red-necked wallabies with water. [2]

Handling & capture

Capture myopathy—muscle damage from pursuit, restraint and transport—is often fatal in wild ungulates. Minimize chase time and stress, avoid hot conditions, use appropriate chemical immobilization by a vet, and release/unload quickly. [3]
